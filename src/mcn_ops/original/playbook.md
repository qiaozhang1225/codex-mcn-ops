# Original short-video writing playbook

Write to earn attention and sustained viewing for this IP's audience. The topic and broad outline are direction, not a rigid script template. The IP's knowledge, viewpoint, and lived experience supply substance. Complete successful works are examples of audience interest and expression; they are not default text to rewrite.

Before choosing an angle, read the most relevant complete sources and any useful notes. Ask what concrete situation the audience recognizes, what they might fear or want, which detail makes the idea vivid, and what the IP can credibly add. These are lenses, not fields every script must contain. Read across contrasting examples when useful; do not copy one work's order, claims, or first-person experience.

Use further research when the knowledge base leaves a material gap. Keep the distinction between sourced knowledge, an example creator's claim, the IP's own experience, and a new inference. If a first-person story is unavailable, write without inventing one. If a factual claim remains uncertain, either verify it or avoid presenting it as certain.

Choose the opening, progression, wording, and ending for this particular topic. A direct judgment, question, scene, tension, or other form may work; none is mandatory. Prefer concrete language that a viewer can follow aloud. Preserve useful complexity where it makes the content distinctive, but translate jargon into accessible speech. A script may be sharp or emotionally resonant without making unsupported identity or outcome claims.

After drafting, reread it as a viewer and as the IP. Remove generic filler, repeated transitions, unearned certainty, and phrases that could fit any account. Check that the main promise of the opening is actually developed. Revise freely; no universal hook count, three-part body, fixed length, or CTA is required. Record the chosen angle and the source IDs actually used. A reviewer may challenge factual support, audience clarity, voice, and originality, but must not force the draft into a template.
